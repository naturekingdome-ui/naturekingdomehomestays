Mullayanagiri is the highest point in Karnataka, at roughly 1,930 m. It is also one of the most accessible high peaks in the state, because a road climbs almost to the summit and the final push is a stepped path rather than a hard trek. That combination makes it the classic Chikmagalur sunrise trip.

Here is what you need to plan it well.

# Getting There

Mullayanagiri is around 20 to 25 km from Chikmagalur town. The route climbs steadily through coffee estates and then open grassland ridges. The road is narrow, with tight bends and a few rough patches near the top, but a regular car handles it with careful driving. Leave extra time in the monsoon, when fog can cut visibility to a few metres.

Many visitors combine Mullayanagiri with Baba Budangiri, about 30 km away across the valley, since the two sit on the same range of hills.

# The Walk to the Summit

From the parking area near the top, a stepped path leads up to the Sri Mullappa Swamy temple at the summit. It is short, but it is steep and the air is thin, so take it slowly if you are not used to altitude. Most reasonably fit people manage it in 15 to 30 minutes. Handrails run along much of the path. In wet weather the steps get slippery, so shoes with grip matter.

There is also a longer ridge walk along Sarpadhari for people who want more of a trek, linking Mullayanagiri with the Baba Budangiri side. This is a proper hike over open, exposed ground, best done with a guide and an early start.

# Sunrise Timing

Sunrise is the reason most people come. To catch it you need to leave Chikmagalur or your estate stay well before dawn, in the dark, allowing around an hour for the drive plus time to park and climb. Check the sunrise time for your dates and work backwards. Aim to be at the top at least 20 minutes early.

On a clear morning the view runs across range after range, with mist pooled in the valleys and the light turning from grey to gold. On a cloudy morning you may see nothing but white, which is the gamble of the monsoon and the early post monsoon weeks.

# Best Season

- November to February: The best odds of a clear sunrise. Cold at the top, sometimes near freezing, so dress for it.
- March to May: Warmer and still mostly clear, though haze can soften the long views.
- June to September: Dramatic and green, but frequently socked in with cloud. Come for the atmosphere, not the panorama, and check whether weather has closed the road.

# What to Carry

- A warm layer, and a windproof one. The summit wind is cold even when the plains are hot.
- Shoes with grip for the steps.
- A torch or headlamp for a pre dawn climb.
- Water and a small snack.
- A rain shell from June to September.
- Cash for parking and any local charges.

# Practical Tips

- Fuel up in Chikmagalur town before you head up. There are no pumps on the peak road.
- Go on a weekday if you can. Weekend sunrises draw a crowd and the small parking area fills fast.
- Carry your trash back down. The grassland ridges litter easily and clean up slowly.
- Network is patchy to absent. Tell someone your plan and download offline maps.
- If you are prone to motion sickness, the bends on the way up are relentless. Sit in front and go easy on breakfast beforehand.

# Making It an Easy Morning

The single biggest thing that makes a Mullayanagiri sunrise pleasant rather than punishing is staying close enough that the pre dawn drive is short. Nature Kingdom sits among coffee plantations near Mallenahalli, within reach of the peak road for an early start, so you are not adding an hour each way from further out. Come back to a bonfire that evening and a slow morning on the terrace with estate coffee. Check in is from 12:00 PM and check out is 11:00 AM, and a BBQ can be arranged on request for an extra charge.

Mullayanagiri rewards an early alarm and a clear sky. Pick your season for the view, or accept the cloud and come for the walk itself.
