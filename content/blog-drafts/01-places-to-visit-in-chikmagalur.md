Chikmagalur is coffee country. The hills here are covered in plantation, the air smells of wet earth and roasting beans, and the roads bend around estates that have been worked by the same families for generations. It is the kind of place where you can watch mist roll off a ridge in the morning and be standing beside a waterfall by afternoon.

This is a local guide to ten places worth your time, with a note on how to reach each one and when it is at its best. Distances are approximate, so check them against a current map before you set out.

# Mullayanagiri Peak

Mullayanagiri is the highest point in Karnataka. A narrow road climbs most of the way up, and a short flight of steps takes you the rest of the distance to a small temple at the summit. On a clear morning you can see range after range fading into the haze. Go at sunrise if you can. The light is soft, the crowds are thin, and the wind at the top is cold enough that a jacket is worth carrying.

# Baba Budangiri

This range sits across the valley from Mullayanagiri and holds the Dattatreya cave shrine, a site visited by both Hindu and Muslim pilgrims. The drive up passes grazing land and open grassland ridges that are good for a slow walk. Manikyadhara Falls, a fine spray of water that pilgrims stand under, is close by.

# Kemmangundi and Z Point

Kemmangundi is an old hill station with a garden laid out by the Mysore royal family. The walk to Z Point ends at a cliff edge with a wide view over the valley, best in the hour before sunset. Kemmangundi also works as a base for Hebbe Falls.

# Hebbe Falls

Hebbe drops in two stages through thick forest near Kemmangundi. The last stretch of the route is rough and done by shared jeep, bouncing through coffee estates and stream crossings. The pool at the base is cold and shallow at the edges. This one is at its most powerful during and just after the monsoon.

# Jhari Falls

Also called Buttermilk Falls, Jhari is reached by a short, steep 4x4 jeep ride from the main road. Water spills over a mossy rock face into a small pool. It is busiest on weekends and quietest on a weekday morning. In the dry months the flow thins out, so time your visit for the greener half of the year.

# Coffee Museum, Chikmagalur Town

If you want the story behind the crop that shaped this district, the Coffee Museum in town lays out how coffee first came to these hills and how it is grown and processed today. It is a small, easy stop and a good primer before an estate walk.

# Hirekolale Lake

A calm lake a short drive from town, ringed by hills, with Mullayanagiri visible in the distance on a clear day. There is not much to do here beyond sit and watch the water, which is the point. Sunset is the time to come.

# Belur and Halebeedu

A little outside Chikmagalur but well worth the drive, these two temple towns hold some of the finest Hoysala stone carving anywhere. The Chennakeshava temple at Belur is still an active place of worship. The detail on the outer walls, rows of dancers, musicians and animals cut into soapstone, rewards a slow look.

# Bhadra Wildlife Sanctuary

On the western edge of the district, Bhadra is home to elephants, gaur, and if you are lucky, a tiger. Safaris and a reservoir boat ride can be arranged through the forest department. Mornings are best for sightings.

# The Coffee Estates Themselves

The best place to visit in Chikmagalur is often just the estate around wherever you are staying. A walk between the coffee rows, under the shade trees where pepper vines climb and birds move through the canopy, gives you the district at its most honest. Ask your host to point you to a path.

# How Many Days Do You Need

You can see the headline sights in two full days if you keep moving. Three days lets you slow down, add a temple town or a wildlife morning, and spend an evening doing nothing at all. Roads here are winding and slower than the distance suggests, so plan fewer stops than you think you can manage.

# Best Time to Go

October to March brings clear air and comfortable days. The monsoon, roughly June to September, turns everything deep green and fills the waterfalls, but some jeep tracks close and leeches come out on forest paths. March and April carry the scent of coffee blossom across the estates after the first rains.

# Where to Stay

Staying on a coffee estate rather than in town puts you closer to the quiet that people come to Chikmagalur for. Nature Kingdom sits among the plantations near Mallenahalli, a short drive from town but far enough that the evenings are silent except for insects and the odd nightjar. Every stay includes a bonfire, and a BBQ can be arranged on request for an extra charge. Check in is from 12:00 PM and check out is 11:00 AM, which leaves room for a slow first afternoon and an unhurried final morning on the terrace with a cup of estate coffee.

Whichever places you choose, build in time to do nothing. Chikmagalur is at its best when you are not rushing through it.
